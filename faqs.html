01 · Getting started

Choosing your appointment.

Do I need to be in pain to benefit?+

No. Physiotherapy is appropriate when pain, injury, rehabilitation or a recurring problem is getting in the way. Physio Life Skills is being developed for people who may not be in pain but want to understand and build the strength, balance and movement that support an active life. Early access is planned for 2027.

Do I need a GP referral?+

No. You can book directly. If your assessment suggests that medical investigation or another professional opinion would be useful, I will explain why and discuss the next step with you.

Should I book physiotherapy or Physio Life Skills?+

Choose physiotherapy now if pain, injury, surgery or a problem that keeps returning is your main concern. Physio Life Skills will offer proactive Strength, Balance and Movement assessments, with early access planned for 2027. You can join the priority list, or email me if you are unsure.

What problems do you commonly help with?+

I commonly see shoulder, arm and elbow pain; back and neck pain; hip and knee problems; foot, ankle and tendon pain; persistent or recurring pain; and rehabilitation after injury or surgery. You do not need to know the exact diagnosis before you come.

Can I come if I have already had scans, surgery or other treatment?+

Yes. Bring any relevant reports, letters or rehabilitation instructions. They can be considered alongside your story and physical assessment rather than viewed in isolation.

Are you professionally registered?+

Yes. Justin is registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC PH54656) and is a member of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy, with nearly 30 years of clinical experience.

02 · Preparing

Before you come.

What should I wear?+

Wear or bring comfortable clothing that lets you move and allows the relevant area to be assessed. Shorts are useful for hip, knee, leg or ankle problems, and a vest or loose top can help with shoulder or neck assessment. You will always be told what an examination involves and can decline or pause at any time.

What should I bring?+

Bring any relevant letters, scan reports, operation or rehabilitation instructions and a list of medication if it may be relevant. It can also help to bring the shoes, brace, walking aid or other equipment connected with the problem—and any questions you want answered.

Can I bring someone with me?+

Yes. You are welcome to bring a relative, friend, carer or chaperone. A parent or legal guardian must attend with anyone under 18.

Where is the clinic, and is there parking?+

The clinic is at Unit 7, Glasshouse Studios, Fryern Court Road, Fordingbridge, Hampshire SP6 1QX. Visitor parking and step-free access are available. See the map and arrival guide before your visit.

What if I have access or mobility concerns?+

Please contact me before your appointment. We can discuss parking, the route into the clinic and anything that would make your visit more comfortable.

Is the clinic an emergency service?+

No. Call 999 for a medical emergency. For urgent advice in England when it is not life-threatening, use NHS 111 online or call 111.

03 · In the room

During your appointment.

What happens at the first appointment?+

An initial physiotherapy appointment lasts 60 minutes. We discuss what has happened, how it affects you and what you would most like to change. A physical assessment follows, then I explain what the findings suggest and we agree a manageable plan. Treatment may begin where appropriate.

Will I be examined or touched?+

Physical assessment and hands-on treatment may be useful, but I will explain what I propose and why before we begin. Your consent is an ongoing conversation: you can ask questions, decline any part or ask to stop at any time.

Will treatment be painful?+

The aim is not to push through pain. Some tests, movements or treatment may temporarily reproduce familiar symptoms, but I will explain what we are looking for and ask for your feedback. Please say if anything feels uncomfortable or worrying.

Will I have hands-on treatment or exercises?+

That depends on what will be most useful for you. Care may combine hands-on treatment, purposeful exercise, activity changes and clear explanation. Each has a different role, and the balance should be personal to you.

Do you use AI-assisted note-taking?+

I may use a secure AI-assisted note-taking tool to help create an accurate clinical record and allow me to focus more fully on our conversation. Before using it, I will explain what it does, how your information is handled and ask for your consent. You can decline or ask me to stop at any time without affecting your care. I remain responsible for checking and approving the final clinical record. Please ask if anything about this is unclear. More detail will be kept in the privacy notice.

What if I do not understand—or I am unhappy with something?+

Please tell me. Ask for a different explanation, more time, a change of approach or a pause. You will not offend me, and speaking up will not affect your care. If something concerns you after the appointment, email me so that it can be addressed.

04 · Next steps

After your appointment.

Will I be given exercises or a plan?+

Often, but only where they serve a clear purpose. The aim is a focused, realistic plan that is understandable and connected to the activities you want to regain—not an overwhelming list.

How many appointments will I need?+

There is no standard course. It depends on the problem, your goals and how things change. I will give you an honest view after assessment and review progress rather than booking treatment by formula. You are not committing to a block of appointments.

What if I feel sore afterwards?+

A short-lived response can sometimes follow assessment, treatment or unfamiliar exercise. I will explain what may be expected and what to do. If symptoms are severe, unusual, getting worse or you feel unwell, seek appropriate medical advice rather than waiting for the next appointment.

Can you write to my GP or consultant?+

Where it would help your care, I can communicate with another healthcare professional with your knowledge and appropriate consent.

05 · Practical details

Fees, cancellations & insurance.

How much does an appointment cost?+

An initial physiotherapy appointment is £120 for 60 minutes and a follow-up is £70 for 30 minutes. Physio Life Skills assessment formats and prices will be confirmed before early access opens in 2027. See the appointments and fees page for current details.

When is payment due?+

Payment is due at the appointment unless it has been taken in advance during online booking.

Do you bill health insurers or third-party providers?+

No. Appointments are self-funded and I do not bill insurance companies or other third-party providers directly. I can provide a receipt for you to submit, but reimbursement depends on your policy. Please check with your insurer before booking, including whether they require a GP referral, pre-authorisation or a particular type of practitioner.

What is the cancellation policy?+

Please give at least 24 hours’ notice if you need to cancel or rearrange. With less than 24 hours’ notice, or if an appointment is missed, the full fee will normally be charged because the time has been reserved for you. If exceptional circumstances prevent you attending, contact the practice as soon as possible. See the full appointment terms.

What happens if I arrive late?+

Arriving late may shorten the available appointment time, and the normal fee will still apply.

06 · Looking ahead

Physio Life Skills.

Is this ordinary physiotherapy?+

It uses the same clinical reasoning, but the starting point is different. Physiotherapy often begins with pain or injury. Physio Life Skills begins with the physical qualities you want to understand, preserve or develop.

Do I need to be fit already?+

No. The assessment meets you where you are. It is not a competition and the purpose is not to compare you with anyone else.

When will I be able to book?+

Early-access appointments are planned for 2027 after the assessments have been carefully developed and trialled. Join the priority list to hear when the first appointments become available.

Will I be able to choose just one assessment?+

Yes. Strength, Balance and Movement are being designed as standalone assessments, so you will be able to begin with the area that matters most. Connections with the other life skills can be discussed without turning the plan into an overwhelming list.

Will I receive exercises?+

Where appropriate, you will receive a focused personal programme with clear demonstrations and a way to record progress. The emphasis is on something realistic enough to use.
